## Teaching with Data, Not Just Intuition

Mathnasium was one of the experiences that most shaped how I communicate technical ideas. Over more than 1,500 hours of STEM instruction, I learned how to break problems down, adapt explanations to different people, and stay accountable to measurable improvement.

I did not treat instruction as a one-size-fits-all process. I used progress data and individualized learning plans to understand where students were struggling and how to adjust the approach for them.

That work translated directly into stronger outcomes, including meaningful improvement in average student performance. More importantly, it built habits I still use in engineering: clarity, patience, feedback loops, and a bias toward observable progress over assumption.

Leading in that environment made me better at mentoring, at presenting ideas, and at designing systems around the people who actually have to use them.
