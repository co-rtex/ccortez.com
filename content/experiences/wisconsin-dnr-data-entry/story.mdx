## Turning Unstructured Inputs into Reliable Data

At the Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources, the work was less about flashy interfaces and more about discipline. The job required taking unstructured or inconsistent submissions and turning them into datasets that other teams could actually use for environmental analysis.

That meant paying attention to validation, consistency, and anomaly detection. I spent time checking records, looking for mismatches, and protecting the integrity of internal systems by making sure the data entering them was dependable.

What I value most about this experience is that it sharpened a part of engineering work people sometimes underestimate: careful operational accuracy. Good systems still fail if the data flowing through them is messy, incomplete, or silently wrong.

This role strengthened my ability to think about data quality as infrastructure. Even when the interface is simple, the reliability requirements are not.
